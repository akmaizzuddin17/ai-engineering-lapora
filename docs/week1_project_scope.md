| **Project Name** | **Lapora** — AI-assisted MBRS XBRL tagging assistant for Malaysian private-company financial statements |
| **Domain** | Accounting & regulatory financial reporting (Malaysia) — SSM Malaysian Business Reporting System (MBRS) |
| **Problem Statement** | Companies in Malaysia that report under MFRS or MPERS must file their financial statements with SSM in XBRL through MBRS. To do this, a preparer has to map every line item in the statements (e.g. *"Trade and other receivables"*, *"Hire purchase payables"*) to the right concept in the SSM Taxonomy (SSMxT). The MPERS part of the taxonomy alone has about 2,375 concepts, and SSM's free mTool gives no automatic mapping. The work is manual, slow, and depends on expertise, and junior preparers or SME accountants often lack that expertise. Keyword search does not work well because company wording rarely matches taxonomy labels exactly, so the task needs semantic matching. AI engineering makes it possible to build a **retrieval-grounded assistant**: it suggests the most likely SSMxT concepts for each line item, cites the taxonomy entry and SSM guidance behind each suggestion, and flags low-confidence items for a human to review. |
| **Evidence of the Problem** | **Evidence 1:** SSM's own FAQ says preparers must map their financial-statement line items to taxonomy concepts, that *"auto population feature for financial statements is not available"*, and that company-specific items with no matching concept must go under generic "Other" elements with footnotes. | **Engineering Gap / Limitation** | **Gap / Limitation:** No existing approach gives Malaysian preparers **grounded, explainable concept suggestions for the SSMxT taxonomy**. The official tool is fully manual. Outsourcing is costly and human-bound. Research systems target US GAAP, and the main benchmark shows that LLMs used alone are unreliable at choosing the exact taxonomy concept. General-purpose chatbots may invent concept names that do not exist in SSMxT. What is missing is a retrieval-grounded pipeline. It should restrict suggestions to real SSMxT concepts, cite the taxonomy definition and SSM guidance for each one, and abstain or flag an item for review when confidence is low. |
| **Initial Project Objectives (2–3)** | **O1:** To design a retrieval-augmented tagging assistant that recommends SSMxT (MPERS) taxonomy concepts for financial-statement line items, using only official SSM and IFRS taxonomy documents as the knowledge source.<br>**O2:** To develop the assistant with a document pipeline (taxonomy labels + SSM guidance → chunks → embeddings → vector store), an LLM ranking step, and guardrails: suggestions limited to valid concept IDs, a citation for every suggestion, low-confidence abstention, and an out-of-scope check.<br>**O3:** To evaluate the assistant on a hand-labelled test set of line items drawn from public illustrative MPERS financial statements, measuring top-1 / top-3 tagging accuracy, groundedness (share of suggestions with a correct citation), invalid-concept rate, abstention quality, and latency. |

| **Similar to EduAdvisor** | Both are **document-grounded RAG systems** built on official, publicly available PDFs: load → clean → chunk → embed → vector store → LLM with retrieved context. Both need **citation-based guardrails** against hallucination and both are evaluated on accuracy, groundedness, latency, and safety. |
| **Different from EduAdvisor** | (1) **Task type:** Lapora is mainly a **classification / ranking task** (choose the correct concept out of about 2,375) rather than open-ended Q&A, so it can be evaluated **objectively** with top-k accuracy against labelled ground truth. (2) **Output constraint:** the answer must be a concept that actually exists in a closed taxonomy, so invalid outputs can be detected automatically. (3) **Input:** the input is structured financial line items (a label and an amount, with statement context), not free-text student questions. (4) **Stakes and users:** the users are professional preparers, and a wrong tag reaches a statutory filing. Human-in-the-loop review is therefore part of the design, not optional. (5) **Domain vocabulary:** company wording differs a lot from taxonomy labels, and English and Bahasa Malaysia labels both exist. |
| **Responsible AI Risks (at least 2)** | **Risk 1 — Hallucination / incorrect tagging:** the model suggests a concept that does not exist, or a plausible but wrong one (e.g. tagging *"Amount due from director"* as trade receivables). The preparer files a misclassified XBRL statement, and the company and its directors, who are legally responsible for the filing, face rejection, re-filing, or misleading public data. *Control:* restrict outputs to valid concept IDs, require a citation, abstain below a confidence threshold, and label every output as a draft for human review.<br><br>**Risk 2 — Automation bias / over-reliance:** junior preparers accept the top suggestion without checking it, so errors spread across many filings and the human review step stops working. *Control:* show confidence scores and alternatives, highlight low-confidence items, and require explicit confirmation before export.<br><br>**Risk 3 — Privacy / confidentiality:** users may paste a client's unpublished financial data into a cloud LLM API, exposing confidential information. *Control:* send only line-item labels (not amounts or company names) to the model, offer a local open-weight model option, and commit no client data to the repository.<br><br>**Risk 4 (fairness / coverage):** retrieval quality may be worse for less common industries (e.g. biological assets in plantations, or construction contract balances) that appear rarely in the guidance documents. Some preparers would then get systematically worse suggestions. *Control:* report accuracy per statement section and per industry in the evaluation. |

## Scope boundaries (Week 1)

- **In scope (this semester):** MPERS full-XBRL financial statements under Companies Act 2016, specifically the *Statement of Financial Position* and *Statement of Profit or Loss and OCI* line items. The output is a ranked concept suggestion list with citations.
- **Out of scope (this semester):** generating the final XBRL instance file, submitting to mPortal, MFRS entities, notes and text-block disclosures, and any judgement about whether the statements themselves are correct. Lapora is a **drafting aid, not accounting or legal advice**. A qualified preparer stays responsible for every filing.
- **Link to FYP:** this semester project is the tagging core of my wider Final Year Project (Lapora: trial balance → financial statements → MBRS XBRL). The FYP extends the pipeline at both ends.
